Hi, I’m Pierre Vernimmen.

PhD student in applied mathematics at UCLouvain, Belgium.

I work on the worst-case analysis and design of first-order optimization methods. In practice, this means that I spend a fair amount of time asking simple-looking questions such as “how bad can gradient descent really be?” and then trying to answer them exactly.

My main tools are performance estimation problems, Lyapunov analysis, semidefinite programming, and computer-assisted proofs. I mostly work on smooth (strongly) convex optimization, exact and inexact gradient methods, and accelerated schemes. I am also interested in stochastic optimization, and their practical implementations.

About

I am a PhD student in applied mathematics at UCLouvain, in the Institute of Information and Communication Technologies, Electronics and Applied Mathematics. My research sits at the intersection of mathematical optimization, numerical analysis, and semidefinite programming.

I like methods that are simple to state but surprisingly subtle to understand. Gradient descent is a perfect example: one line of code, many possible convergence measures, and a lot of interesting mathematics hiding behind the constants.

Research

A large part of my work is based on the Performance Estimation Problem methodology. The idea is to formulate the worst-case behavior of an optimization algorithm as another optimization problem. The numerical solution gives a sharp guarantee, and the dual certificate often contains the proof we were looking for.

I am particularly interested in tight convergence guarantees, gradient-norm bounds, robust step-size schedules, relatively inexact gradients, and the way numerical certificates can be transformed into clean analytical arguments.
